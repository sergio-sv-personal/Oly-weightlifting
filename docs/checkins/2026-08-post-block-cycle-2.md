# Post Block Check In / Review — Cycle 2

Submitted via Google Forms during the deload week after cycle 2 (block: 11 May – early Aug 2026).
Coach: Seb. Submitted by: Sergio Suarez, early August 2026.

---

**How successful do you feel this last block was? What things are you looking at when you are measuring success?**

Mixed feelings, honestly. On the numbers the block was clearly successful — snatch went 40 → 44, I tested clean & jerk for the first time at 65 (I'd never jerked more than 57 until the last week), push press went 50 → 60, and cleans went from struggling at 50 to comfortable doubles at 60. I also fixed real technique problems along the way: my hips stopping shooting early in the pull, my lats finally engaging after I widened my stance, and finding the glute-engagement cue that fixed my split.

But it didn't always feel successful, and that's what I'm measuring: how the lifts feel, not just the load. Every fault I fix exposes a new one, and the current ones feel less like technique and more like physical limits — my overhead position is unstable (wrists won't settle back, lockout feels wrong), the split depends on strength/mobility I don't have yet (back leg, glutes, getting in faster), and I'm not sure I break parallel on heavy squats. So going into next block I feel limited by strength and mobility more than by understanding — I mostly know what I should do now; my body can't always do it yet.

**What areas do you feel you would like to focus on improving next block?**

1. The overhead position — wrist and shoulder mobility plus stability. It's the wall the snatch is currently against, and it feels physical, not conceptual.
2. The split — making the glute engagement automatic and the footwork faster. I found what works in the last week of the block; next block is where it needs to become habit.
3. Bracing. A pattern I noticed across lifts this block: I inflate my rib cage instead of bracing 360° into my core — it showed up in the drop snatch, and my heavy squats (core not engaged, hips twisting at 100). I'd like to actually learn to brace properly.

**What exercises did you enjoy this last block? Why?**

The cleans, by a distance — they're the lift that clicked this block and went from a struggle at 50 to comfortable doubles at 60. I genuinely look forward to them now.

**What exercises did you find challenging this last block? Why? Would you like to keep working towards these exercises?**

The snatch was the hardest thing all block — but in an evolving way. First it was the starting position and my hips shooting early; once those improved, the challenge moved to the top: my overhead position is unstable, my wrists don't settle back, and the third-pull rhythm (elbow → arm → wrist) isn't there yet. It cost me reps at 37 even in the last week. Yes — absolutely keep working toward it; it's the lift with the most left to unlock, and I feel like the fixes are stacking up in the right order.

The jerk was the other big one. The dip drifting forward got mostly fixed with "head to the ceiling", but the split has been the stubborn fault: glutes not engaging, back leg not bending, weight landing on the front foot, and soft press-outs at the top end. I only found the cue that works (consciously switching the glute on) in the final week — so I'd really like next block to build on that while it's fresh.

**What are your short term goals that you would like to be working towards on your upcoming training block?**

1. A stable overhead position in the snatch — letting the wrists settle back, trusting the lockout, and bringing the wider-stance setup that fixed my pulls into the snatch itself. I'd love for 40 to become a comfortable, repeatable working weight rather than a good day (I made 44 on test day, but the singles at 37 still feel unreliable overhead).
2. Making the glute engagement in the split automatic instead of something I have to consciously switch on, and getting into the split faster. Success would be jerking 60+ without any press-out.
3. The strength/mobility base under both of those: overhead and wrist mobility, and squat depth I can trust — I want to be certain I'm breaking parallel at 100+, and I'd like to actually test my back squat.

**What are your medium (1-2 years) term goals that you would like to be working towards overall with your training?**

1. The classic milestones: a bodyweight clean & jerk (82kg — I'm at 65 now) and a snatch around 60 within the next year or two. Ambitious from where I am :(
2. Technique I can trust under load: a snatch I receive deep and stable without thinking about my wrists, and a jerk where the split is automatic. I want the lifts to feel like one motion, not a checklist of cues.
3. Enter a local competition at some point in the next year — nothing serious, just to put the lifts on a platform and have a reason to train the full lifts to a peak.
4. Be fully RX in classes: double unders, toes-to-bar, chest to bar, etc and the engine/pacing to match — so the CrossFit side stops being a scaled version of the workout.
5. Above all: still training 5–6 days a week in two years, uninjured. Everything else depends on that.

**Did you hit any PBs this block? If so what are they?**

44 snatch and 64 (or 65 CJ)

**How is your body feeling currently? Is there anywhere you are concerned about or feel needs attention?**

The main concern: my left knee. I've got ITB-related pain there and it's been getting steadily worse — it's not going away on its own, and the trend is the wrong direction.

Beyond that, nothing acute, but two things worth naming: my wrists and overhead position feel like a mobility limiter in the snatch (the wrists don't want to settle back under the bar), and my lower back lets me know when form breaks on high-rep pulls in metcons / class. Recovery in general has been okay but rarely great — my Whoop has sat mostly in the yellow (mid-50s to low-60s recovery) all block.

**Do you have any questions or feedback for your coach that you would like to share?**

Mostly appreciation: the cues that landed ("head to the ceiling", "elbows to the sky", staying over the bar) each fixed something real, and the way the block progressed things (pauses shrinking until the full lifts appeared) made sense in my body, not just on paper. Keep doing that.

**Health Habits: How successful has your fuelling for training been and your post training fuelling? Do you feel like this is an area you would like to improve on or need support on?**

Big picture: I've lost about 20kg in the last year (peaked at 103.6, now around 80) — so my fuelling has mostly been in service of weight loss, and on that front it's been very successful. But I suspect it's now the thing holding my training back: I usually do the 6am sessions fasted (it cost me badly once this block — I skipped breakfast and felt dizzy and weak the whole session), my recovery scores sit permanently in the yellow, and I finished the block feeling more limited by strength than by technique — which makes sense if I've been under-fuelled for a year.

So yes, this is the area I'd most like support on. I'm close to where I want to be weight-wise, and I'd like help transitioning from "eating to lose" to "eating to perform" — what maintenance looks like for me, what's realistic to eat before a 6am class, and what my post-training fuelling should be now that I'm at 5–6 sessions a week.

I know you offer a dedicated nutrition session — I'm happy to book and pay for that if you think it would help. Just tell me and we'll set it up.

**Health Habits: How has your sleep been this block? Is this an area you feel you could improve on or would like some support on?**

Middling, and I have the data: my Whoop says I averaged about 6 hours 50 minutes across the block, with sleep performance around 80%. Roughly one night in five was under 6 hours, and I only cracked 7+ hours on about half the nights. The short nights cluster before the 6am classes — the early alarm is what caps me.

Yes, it's an area I could improve, and it's probably connected to the recovery scores sitting in the yellow all block. The honest fix is an earlier bedtime before the 6am days rather than anything sophisticated. If you have a view on whether the 6am sessions are worth the sleep cost versus shifting some training later, I'd take that input — otherwise this one's on me to tighten up.
